Liquidity

Deposit both sides, then confirm — each side is its own transaction because a transaction carries one currency. Anything deposited but not yet confirmed is always refundable.

The first person to supply a market sets its opening price. After that both sides must go in at the market's current ratio — anything left over stays yours and can be taken back.

Open a pool

Pair any token with NADO. Opening is permissionless and free — the pool is empty until someone adds liquidity.

Cross-chain order book

Swap NADO against BTC / ETH with no custodian and no wrapped coins: both sides lock behind the same secret (an atomic swap). The NADO side escrows in a contract that can only ever pay the funder back (cancel / expiry) or the counterparty against the secret — nobody else, ever. The foreign side is a standard HTLC you create on that chain with the hashlock shown here.

Post an order

Posting creates your swap secret (kept in this browser — back it up below). Selling NADO locks it in the contract now; buying NADO locks nothing until someone fills. Whatever happens, an unfinished order refunds to whoever paid in once it expires — nothing can get stuck. After posting, the order's row offers Deposit… to make takers stake a returnable good-faith deposit, and Tip to pay watchtowers to finish the swap for you.

Limit orders

Name your own price instead of taking the pool's: say what you give and what you want, and the first person to accept swaps both sides in one go — no partial fills. Cancel any time before someone takes it; after it expires anyone can return it to you.

Every pool and swap is public and verifiable on-chain. Supplying liquidity carries risk: if the price moves, withdrawing gives you back a different mix of the two than you put in. Nothing here is investment advice.
